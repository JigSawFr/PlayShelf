# The website

PlayShelf's website, on GitHub Pages: <https://shelf.boreale.dev/>.

- **A home page and the documentation** in the console's 15 languages, one catalog each in `site/i18n/`: English at the root, the others in their folder (`/fr/`, `/fr-ca/`, `/de/`, `/es/`, `/es-419/`, `/it/`, `/nl/`, `/pt/`, `/pt-br/`, `/ru/`, `/ja/`, `/ko/`, `/zh-hans/`, `/zh-hant/`). A first visit to the root from elsewhere goes to the browser's language when the site has it; the header's language button opens their menu (a popover, as the menu of Boréale's sites), each language with its flag before its name (Mexico's for Latin America's Spanish, Taiwan's for Traditional Chinese; `FLAGS` in `build_site.py`), and the one chosen is remembered. The documentation is in English and French: the other languages show the English pages, with a note saying so (below). Lines break as each script wants (site.css): Japanese headings and short lines between phrases, Korean between words, Chinese with no punctuation opening a line and its titles whole between their punctuation. One dark look, Boréale's (the night, the aurora: [boreale/branding](https://github.com/JigSawFr/boreale/tree/main/branding)), as [Aurore's site](https://aurore.boreale.dev/) and [boreale.dev](https://boreale.dev/); the web simulator keeps its own theme.
- **The home page**: the aurora behind its header and hero, then what PlayShelf does, its screens, the simulator, how to install it and start, the questions (each with its own address, `#faq-<question>`), the documentation's pages. The screenshots open larger over the page (`site.js`: swipe or the arrows, down or Esc to close), the documentation's too.
- **The home page's showcase** (site.css's and site.js's "showcase" blocks, the same on the four sites; PlayGuard's is the reference): the Catalogue on a console of our own (`.device`, its sides `DEVICE_GRIPS`), leaning towards the text on a wide screen; the night going on down the page (veils of aurora, faint stars, a horizon over the footer); the features on tiles, their edge lit under the pointer, the three first showing their screen at the card's foot (the security, which has none, its shield large); the sections rising into view once; on a wide screen, the screens' gallery as one large over a strip of the others; the steps joined by a line; a short cross-fade from one page to the next where the browser can. Without script, or with less motion asked, everything stays in place. The header, the hero, the sections and the footer share one edge: 1180px of content between the gutters.
- **Where PlayShelf stands**, until its first release: one panel under the hero says it is not usable yet, what works, what is under way and what comes next (`ROADMAP` in `build_site.py`, as the README's warning says it: keep them in step), and, quieter, that it is for one's own content only (a callout of its own once released).
- **The screenshots in each language**, in the console's dark theme, as the site is: its screens as the simulator shows them (below); the English ones of `images/screenshots/` for a local build without them.
- **The documentation** is `docs/`, converted from its Markdown at each build (without its "Back to the README", GitHub's way back): each language reads `docs/<language>/` where it has the page (Canadian French reads `docs/fr/`), else the English page, with a note saying it is not translated yet. A translation's first line, `<!-- translated from docs/<page>.md, sha256 <12 hex> -->` (`tools/doc_stamp.py docs/<language>/<page>.md`), is the English page it matches: once that page changes, the site says under the translation that the English one may be more recent. GitHub's `> [!WARNING]` blocks become callouts, headings keep GitHub's anchors, links between the docs and to the README become the site's pages, and any other file of the repository links to GitHub. A translation keeps the English page's sections, in the same order.
- **Boréale's sites**, in a menu the header's name opens, the site's own leading home (`switcher()` in `build_site.py`, a popover the browser closes on Esc or a click elsewhere): boreale.dev, the homebrew, the libraries, each in the page's language. The four sites share the same 15 folders (English at the root, then `fr`, `fr-ca`, `de`, `es`, `es-419`, `it`, `nl`, `pt`, `pt-br`, `ru`, `ja`, `ko`, `zh-hans`, `zh-hant`), so a link to another site, the footer's included (boreale.dev, PlayGuard, Aurore, the privacy and legal notice at `boreale.dev/<folder>/legal/`), goes to its page in the same folder. Its CSS and script are the "site switcher" blocks of boreale.dev's `site.css` and `site.js` (the reference), and the marks are boreale's (`site/assets/marks/`).
- **The menu and the footer**: the menu of Boréale's sites shows each released project's latest version (boreale.dev's `versions.json`, read at the build; none for a local build offline), and the footer has a Boréale column: the other sites, the privacy and legal notice, `contact@boreale.dev`. The page not found stands under the aurora, as the home page's hero.
- **Reading the documentation**: once the header is scrolled away, a slim bar at the top (the way home, the page's name, the search, the light reading); *Light reading* puts the documentation's pages on a light ground (kept as `ps-reading`, `assets/reading.js` setting it before the first paint); the search's field shows its key, `/`.
- **The documentation's search**: a box above its pages, over `docs/search.json` (each section of the language's pages and the home page's questions).
- **The changelog** (`/changelog/`, `CHANGELOG.md`, its notes in English) and `releases.atom`, a feed of the releases. Once there is a release, the documentation comes in two versions: the latest release's, out of its tag, at `docs/`, and main's at `docs/dev/`, with a switch between them.
- **The page not found** (`404.html`, served at any address) in every language.
- **For search engines and AI assistants**: absolute links, `sitemap.xml`, structured data (schema.org JSON-LD), a preview image (`.github/readme/social-preview.png`), each page of the documentation in Markdown next to it, listed in [`llms.txt`](https://llmstxt.org) (`llms-full.txt`: the whole documentation in one file). A pull request's preview says `noindex`.
- **On a phone**: no field under 16px (iOS zooms into one), 44px to touch, the page beside the notch in landscape, nothing wider than the screen.
- **The web simulator** in `/simulator/` ([`web/README.md`](../web/README.md)).
- **Each open pull request's preview** in `/pr-<number>/`: its website and its simulator, linked in a comment on the pull request.

## How it is made

`tools/build_site.py <out-dir>` (Python 3 and Python-Markdown) writes the pages; no framework, nothing to install but that.

- `site/i18n/<language>.json`: the home page's and the header's texts, one catalog for each of the console's 15 languages (`en-US.json` the reference: a text missing from another catalog is shown in English). A new text goes in all 15, translated, its names of screens and menus as the app's own catalog has them; product names, file names, commands and addresses stay as they are. The site is built in the languages that have one: a new language is a catalog here, and its pages in `docs/<language>/`. In a text, `{{app:<key>}}` is the app's own text for that key in that language (`resources/i18n/<language>/playshelf.json`), so a menu the site names is named as on the console; `{{version}}` is the latest release (`.release-please-manifest.json`).
- `site/assets/`: `site.css` (Boréale's colours), `site.js` (the home page's aurora, boreale.dev's own, still when less motion is asked; the language menu, the lightbox, the search, the copy buttons) and the fonts (Sora for the headings, Figtree for the text, JetBrains Mono for code and labels, SIL Open Font License, `fonts/OFL-*.txt`; the language menu's 15 flags cut out of Twemoji's, CC-BY 4.0, `fonts/LICENSE-twemoji-flags.txt`, so that every system draws the same, Windows included), served with the site so that no visitor's browser loads anything from anyone else.
- **The screenshots**: `tools/site_screenshots.py <simulator> <out-dir> [language…]` (Playwright for Python) runs the web simulator in a headless Chromium and saves each screen of the gallery in each language, in the dark theme the site shows, `<out-dir>/<language>/<screen>-dark.webp`, on the smoke test's console with a game of the demo server installed on the way. `SITE_SCREENSHOTS=<out-dir>` tells `build_site.py` to use them. With `--png`, it also takes the English ones in the light theme and writes them to `images/screenshots/`, which the documentation shows on GitHub. A new screen is a name in `SCREENS` and the keys that lead to it.
- **The site's address**: `SITE_URL` (`https://shelf.boreale.dev/` by default). CI asks GitHub Pages for it (its custom domain) and adds a preview's folder; `SITE_BASE` is its path, for 404.html; `SITE_NOINDEX=1` for a preview.
- **The audience**, counted by Umami Cloud (<https://cloud.umami.is>): `assets/umami.js` is its tracker, served with the site, that sends each page seen to `gateway.umami.is`, without cookie, and only from the site's own address (`SITE_URL`'s, not a local copy); a preview has none. It is a copy: take it again from <https://cloud.umami.is/script.js> when Umami changes it. Its site in Umami (`UMAMI_WEBSITE` in `build_site.py`) is Boréale's, shared with the other projects' sites: Umami's *Hostname* filter tells them apart.
- **A Content-Security-Policy** on every page (a `<meta>`: GitHub Pages sends no headers): only the site's own files, no inline script or style; the one address it may send to is Umami's (`connect-src`). A page needing something else is blocked: put it in `site/assets/`.

To look at it locally: `make site` (the simulator in it once `make web` built it), then <http://localhost:8000/>. With the screenshots in each language: `python3 tools/site_screenshots.py build-web/simulator /tmp/shots`, then `SITE_SCREENSHOTS=/tmp/shots python3 tools/build_site.py /tmp/site`.

## Checks

- `tools/check_site_links.py <site> [site-url]`: every link of the site to the site, and every `#anchor`, leads somewhere.
- `tools/site_layout.py <site>` (Playwright for Python): no page wider than the screen on a phone, a tablet or a desktop, no field iOS zooms into, no script that throws, and nothing axe-core finds against WCAG 2.2 AA (`SITE_AXE=<axe.min.js>`).

## Publishing

`.github/workflows/web.yml` builds the simulator, its smoke test, the screenshots and the site with the simulator in it for main and for each pull request (the `site` artifact, kept 30 days), and runs both checks. `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes them, with GitHub Actions as the source of GitHub Pages: main's at the root, each open pull request's in `pr-<number>/` (not a fork's: its pages would be served from this site), then edits the pull request's comment with the links. It runs where main's workflows run (after each `web` run, when a pull request is closed, or by hand), as the `github-pages` environment requires, and only puts in place the files `web.yml` built. Each run publishes everything again, so a closed pull request's preview goes at the next one.

GitHub lists a workflow's runs by branch *name*, whatever repository the branch is in: `pages.yml` takes only runs of this repository (`head_repository`), so a fork can never have its files served from the site.

Once: *Settings › Pages › Build and deployment › Source: GitHub Actions*.
